## Features and effects

### F1: A restrained station assembly on browser load

- Problem and current behavior: the complete station appears immediately, with overview framing applied on scene revisions.
- Proposed behavior: mailroom powers up first, observatory follows, then project rooms appear outward along their connections. Each room has an outline, a solid floor, furnishings and crew, and one restrained rim sweep. A continuous eased camera pullback finishes at the normal overview in 2.8 seconds. Large stations reveal groups within that same duration.
- Effect on the code: a browser-view presentation controller coordinates painting and camera framing. The scene builder, layout, movement identities and data remain complete throughout.
- Scope and compatibility: one attempt per mounted page, including reload; no replay for polls, filters, deck changes, theme resources or visibility resume. Supported station and office art use the same presentation schedule. Existing fallback art remains supported. No bounce, flashes, fabricated activity, dependencies or server configuration.
- Acceptance outcomes: progression is coherent across cached surfaces, tall wall props, live furniture, crew, labels, effects and interaction targets. Empty but valid stations reveal the two hubs and finish normally. Final output matches ordinary overview.
- Execution mapping: Tasks 2 and 3.

### F2: Immediate control and reduced-motion behavior

- Proposed behavior: pan, zoom and selection complete the reveal before applying the requested action. Sidebar remains usable. Reduced motion skips or finishes the reveal immediately. Resize and scene revisions finish the reveal rather than restarting or competing with its camera.
- Effect on the code: input and camera entry points share a synchronous completion operation; lifecycle owns cancellation and frame scheduling.
- Acceptance outcomes: no delayed camera override, invisible actionable objects, replay after resume, or pending frames after stop. Failed or absent observations do not consume the first real-snapshot gate.
- Execution mapping: Tasks 2 and 3.

### F3: Saved visual settings in one menu

- Problem and current behavior: Ambient FX has a standalone toolbar button with guarded viewer storage and theme-aware defaults.
- Proposed behavior: one **Visual settings** popover contains independent **Startup animation** and **Ambient FX** switches. Startup animation defaults on; reduced motion always overrides it. Existing `observer.fx` values and defaults retain their meaning. Enabling startup animation after an attempt does not replay it; disabling during playback completes it immediately.
- Effect on the code: reuse preferences and persistent listener ownership; move FX into the popover and add one preference. No additional options.
- Acceptance outcomes: switches expose checked states, support keyboard activation, survive reload, and work when storage throws. Escape closes and restores trigger focus; outside click and focus leaving dismiss; controls work at narrow viewport widths.
- Execution mapping: Tasks 1 and 3.

## High-level design and rationale

Keep presentation separate from scene construction. A small `StartupAssembly` controller owns pending/playing/complete state, stable room ordering, timing and camera interpolation. It never removes workers or platforms from `Scene`. Use one schedule and one completion operation rather than multiple room timers.

Painting must be owner-aware, not a whole-canvas floor-shaped clip: office wall art, telescopes and crew extend above and outside floor polygons. Add a presentation hook to existing static painter calls, and associate live items and labels with their owning hub or platform. Preserve current static ordering and the global dynamic depth sort. Apply progress to whole draw operations; never crop tall objects to a room footprint. Floor stages fade into their solid form, while furnishing stages reveal complete object silhouettes. For static painters that combine floor and props (mail hub), separate those two existing responsibilities without duplicating their geometry.

During the short intro only, paint the owner-aware static pass directly to the existing context. After completion use the existing `Cache.staticFor()` unchanged. This avoids per-room full-station canvases and frame-by-frame cache invalidation. Direct rendering cost must be measured with the existing synthetic large-station fixture before accepting this choice; do not introduce another cache framework speculatively. Optional status pools must honor worker ownership too. Preserve the normal painter path when no reveal hook is provided.

Use a non-modal settings popover with ordinary Tab navigation and buttons with `role="switch"` and `aria-checked`. Do not apply ARIA menu roles that would require a different arrow-key interaction model. Reuse help-popover dismissal conventions and existing `NodeEvents`; keep help and settings mutually exclusive.

- Risks and safeguards: overlap is addressed through draw ownership and preserved ordering; real source revisions complete rather than remap an active schedule; async art settlement completes rather than swaps art mid-reveal; input completes synchronously before hit testing. Guard all storage access with existing preferences. No credentials, source records or labels enter preference storage.
- Design tradeoff: completing early on resize or genuine geometry revision favors current data and responsive control over finishing decorative motion. Ordinary equal-revision polls continue without restarting or reframing the intro.
